## 1. The discovery principle: real language over gut feel

The single biggest factor in messaging quality is whether the client pulls from real sources versus answering from gut feel. Real sources to mine:

- Sales call recordings or notes — the words prospects use to describe their problem
- Customer support emails and DMs — the friction points that come up repeatedly
- Reviews and testimonials — the outcomes clients actually credit
- LinkedIn comments and replies — how the audience talks when they don't think anyone's listening
- Survey responses — direct quotes usable almost verbatim

If a real quote can be copy-pasted into an answer, that's gold. Real language beats imagined language every single time. If the client lacks these sources, the process still works — but flag thin sections rather than papering over them.

## 2. Persona naming

The persona gets a name and a one-line backstory. "Growth-Stage Greg runs a $750K agency stuck at a referral ceiling" beats "our ideal customer is a service-business owner" — every downstream messaging decision gets sharper when writing to a person instead of a category.

Model persona (Growth-Stage Greg):

- 38, runs a 6-person creative agency doing ~$750K/year
- Got there on referrals — can't crack $1M because the pipeline is unpredictable
- Embarrassed by his current website but doesn't know exactly what to fix
- Wants to look as polished as the agencies he envies on LinkedIn

All psychographic answers are written in the persona's voice ("Greg wants...", not "our ideal customer wants...").

## 3. Workbook structure (Parts 1–7)

**Part 1 — Who Are They? (Demographics)**

- Basic profile: role/title, industry, company size and revenue (B2C: age, income, life stage)
- Decision-making power: final decision-maker vs. influencer; who else is involved
- Where they spend time: platforms, publications, where they look for solutions
- Ends with a **Persona Snapshot (Demographics)**: 2–3 sentences making the raw answers a person

**Part 2 — What Do They Care About? (Psychographics)**

- Goals & Aspirations: business goals (6–12 months), personal desires (what they want to feel), status & identity (how they want to be perceived)
- Problems & Pain Points: current frustrations, costs of inaction, failed attempts (and why those failures bred skepticism)
- Objections & Barriers: common objections and pre-purchase questions, internal barriers (budget, time, fear of wrong choice), competitive considerations
- Values & Beliefs: decision principles, non-negotiables, relationship expectations (vendor vs. partner, communication style)
- Ends with a **Persona Snapshot (Psychographics)** written in the persona's first-person voice

**Part 3 — What Language Do They Use?**

- How they describe their problem (exact phrases)
- How they describe success
- Common questions they ask
- Ends with **Your Persona's Phrases**: 5–10 verbatim phrases, no cleanup, no marketing polish. These are the raw material for soundbites.

**Part 4 — Customer Research & Validation**

- Best customers (3–5) and what they have in common
- Customer interviews (why chose us, what almost stopped them, what changed)
- Review/testimonial themes
- Competitive research: how competitors position, what's saturated, the gap to own, why customers leave competitors

**Part 5 — Understanding Value Depth** (see section 4 below)

**Part 6 — Messaging Raw Material**

- Plain-English description: what the client does, said out loud to a friend (not a tagline)
- Who it's absolutely perfect for / absolutely not for
- What changes for clients: life six months later, the most concrete deliverable, the unexpected gain
- How the client talks about the work: the misconception they're tired of correcting, the hill-to-die-on industry belief, the one homepage idea
- Proof and results: impressive numbers, testimonial themes that surprise them

**Part 7 — Synthesis** (filled by the sprint, not the client)

- Persona Profile, ICP One-Pager, and M2M Messaging One-Pager — copied in from the sprint so the workbook stands alone

## 4. Value Depth

Services deliver value at different levels — some functional (the thing gets done), some transformational (who the customer becomes). The deeper the value, the more the messaging speaks to identity and transformation, not deliverables. The five probes:

1. **Business Impact** — does the service grow revenue, scale the business, improve outcomes?
2. **Confidence & Clarity** — do customers gain confidence, articulate what was fuzzy, feel certain about decisions?
3. **Status & Credibility** — does it raise their standing, make them more credible, let them compete with bigger players?
4. **Skills & Capability** — do they keep frameworks/skills long after the engagement?
5. **Identity & Purpose** — do they become the version of themselves they aspire to be?

The answers produce the **Value Depth Summary** on the ICP One-Pager (where the service sits on the functional → transformational spectrum + the key transformation), which calibrates how deep the Change and End Result soundbites go.

## 5. Brand Commitment Ladder

Based on Marty Neumeier's framework and Maslow's hierarchy. Used to calibrate messaging depth per surface:

- **Level 1: Satisfaction** — delivered what was promised, on time. "We built your website on schedule and on budget."
- **Level 2: Delight** — exceeded expectations. "We delivered early, included bonus training, and your site loads faster than competitors."
- **Level 3: Engagement** — the customer feels part of a tribe. "You're part of our community of founders who refuse to settle for generic positioning."
- **Level 4: Empowerment** — personal growth, confidence, status, identity. "You gained the clarity and confidence to lead your market and command premium pricing."

Application: cold traffic = Level 1–2; homepage hero = Level 2–3 (or 3 when traffic is mostly warm/referral); About page and sales conversations = Level 4; premium offers must justify pricing with Level 4 transformation.

## 6. Sprint outputs the workbook feeds

| Workbook input                            | Sprint output it powers                           |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Pain points + problem language            | Problem soundbite (all three levels)              |
| Psychographics (feelings, self-talk)      | Empathy soundbite                                 |
| Why They Choose You / differentiation     | Answer soundbite                                  |
| Goals, status & identity                  | Change soundbite → usually the Controlling Idea   |
| Success language                          | End Result soundbite                              |
| Costs of inaction + competitive gap       | Survival liability/asset → Tagline                |
| Plain-English description + point of view | Business Story voice                              |
| Where they spend time                     | Deployment Map surfaces                           |
| Value Depth answers                       | Value Depth Summary → soundbite depth calibration |
