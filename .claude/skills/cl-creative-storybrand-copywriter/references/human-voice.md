# Human voice: what reads as AI, and what to write instead

Casey's rules, from line-by-line feedback on the JC's Environmental
Technologies demo (Nov 19 2026). They apply to every page of every client.
Load this before writing, and check every line against it before showing
Casey anything. The test for each line: **would the owner say this,
out loud, to a customer standing in their driveway?** If not, rewrite it.

## The tells Casey rejects

### 1. A slogan angle built from a virtue

Words like "honest answer", "straight answer", "tell you straight", "no
surprises", "peace of mind" or "we'll be upfront", built into a brand
angle. They read as AI the moment they repeat. A customer can say "he was
honest" in a review; the company saying it about itself, over and over,
is a tell.

- ✗ Asbestos, mold & lead removal, _with an honest answer first_
- ✓ Mold, asbestos and lead removal in Dallas–Fort Worth, _by a licensed
  Texas crew since 1996_

Show the virtue with a fact or a customer's own quote, never by naming it.

### 2. Summing-up sentences that start "You get…"

- ✗ You get the photos and paperwork your insurer or buyer will ask for.
- ✓ …then document the job with photos for your insurer or buyer.
- ✗ You pay the rest when the job is done, and get the project photos.
- ✓ The rest is due when the job is done. We send the project photos and
  all required documentation.

### 3. Tidy benefit tails on the end of a sentence

End on the fact. Cut the little flourish that explains why it's good.

- ✗ …and deal with your insurer directly so you're not stuck in the middle.
- ✓ Then we repair the damage and work directly with your insurance
  company.
- ✗ Found with a thermal camera, not guesswork.
- ✓ Found with a thermal camera.
- ✗ "start to finish", "as soon as it's safe", "so you know what you're
  dealing with", "the part that prevents a second job"

### 4. The three-part list cadence

"Clear communication, thorough work, and a crew that cleans up before it
leaves." The message can be right (here it was: people do wonder who
cleans up), but the rhythm of three is what AI does. Break it into one
plain sentence, or let a customer's real words carry the point.

- ✓ Either one can stop a project. We seal off the area, take it out, and
  clean up before we leave.
- ✓ Then quote the customer: "…did such a great job sealing off the
  kitchen, removing the tile and asbestos and cleaning up afterwards."

The same goes for triplet headlines ("What we remove, restore and
rebuild").

### 5. Abstract or mood headlines

- ✗ Calm, careful work _when something worrying turns up._
- ✗ When mold or asbestos turns up, _we take it from here._
- ✓ Mold behind a shower wall. Asbestos under old tile. _We've handled
  both since 1996._

Name the thing, the place and the fact.

### 6. Phrases nobody actually says

Read it aloud. Office-speak and verbs that sound like a manual are out.

- ✗ Call or send the form.
- ✓ Give us a call or fill out the form.
- ✗ After the cleanup, we manage the trades and put the space back.
- ✓ We rebuild what had to come out, and hire and manage the
  subcontractors for you.

### 7. Counting things for no reason

- ✗ Six services for homes and commercial buildings across Dallas–Fort
  Worth.
- ✓ For homeowners, builders and businesses, from the first inspection to
  the rebuild.

Say who the services are for and where. A count ("six services", "four
steps") reads as forced unless the number itself matters to the reader.

### 8. The usual AI vocabulary

Never use:

- seamless
- peace of mind
- comprehensive
- ensure
- dedicated
- tailored
- top-notch
- cutting-edge
- exceptional service
- daunting
- "not just X, but Y"
- "whether you're X or Y"
- "Our mission is simple:"
- colon-label constructions ("Label: explanation") in body copy
- em-dash flourishes

## What good sounds like

- **Short, concrete sentences.** Subject, verb, fact.
- **The real nouns:** the old kitchen tile, the shower wall, the leak
  upstairs, the county names.
- **"We" for the company and "you" for the customer.** No company-as-hero
  boasting.
- **The virtue lives in the evidence:** the year, the license, the
  customer's own words.
- **Contractions, the way people talk:** "we'll", "it's", "you're".
- **Every claim traceable** to the client's site, their Google profile or
  a review. Nothing invented, nothing stretched (no "free inspection" when
  they only offer a free quote).

## The check before Casey sees it

1. Read every line aloud against the driveway test.
2. Search the draft for each tell above ("honest", "straight", "You get",
   "so you", "start to finish", "not just", "whether you", "six/four …").
3. Hand the copy plus the client's research files to a fresh reviewer
   subagent. Give it these rules and the facts, not your own opinion. Ask
   it for every line that is unsupported or reads as AI, each with a
   rewrite.
4. Fix everything it finds before showing Casey.
