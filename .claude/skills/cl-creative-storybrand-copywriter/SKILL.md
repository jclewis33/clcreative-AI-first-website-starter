# CL Creative StoryBrand Website Copywriter

Generate high-converting website copy for CL Creative clients using the M2M (Market to Message) Framework, structured around the StoryBrand 7-beat story arc.

## Core Methodology

This skill combines:

1. **StoryBrand 7-beat story arc** - The structural spine of every full-page copy job
2. **Locked M2M messaging** - When a Messaging Sprint deliverable exists, its soundbites are quoted verbatim (see Locked Messaging Mode)
3. **M2M Framework insights** - ICP demographics, psychographics, pain points, goals, language
4. **Conversion optimization** - Brand Commitment Ladder, strategic CTAs, buyer journey alignment
5. **SEO best practices** - Keyword integration, on-page structure, meta elements

## Input Priority

The usual input now is **research**, not a questionnaire: Casey rarely sends
clients the M2M questionnaire anymore. Write from what the business has
already put into the world, and hold every line to `references/human-voice.md`
whatever the input.

1. **Default: research on the business.** Their current site (every page
   saved as text), their Google Business Profile (categories, services,
   review topic chips, hours), and every written review. The reviews are the
   best source of the customer's own language and of what sets the business
   apart. Use their words, not adjectives about them. Every claim traces to
   one of these; anything inferred goes to the Open Items list.
2. **Bonus: a completed M2M Messaging Sprint.** If one exists (locked PEACE
   soundbites, Controlling Idea, Decision Trigger, Tagline, Business Story,
   ICP One-Pager), it switches on **Locked Messaging Mode** below and outranks
   everything else.
3. **Also useful: an M2M/ICP questionnaire or competitor sites.** These add
   ICP insight, or the angles and gaps to write against. Neither one is
   required.

Load `references/m2m-framework.md` only when sprint or questionnaire material
is in play.

## Locked Messaging Mode

When the client has a finished Messaging Sprint, that deliverable is **scripture**. This mode produced CL Creative's best copy output to date (a youth cheer competition client): follow it exactly.

**The verbatim rule.** The 5 PEACE soundbites, Controlling Idea, Tagline, and Decision Trigger are quoted **word for word, never reworded, paraphrased, or "improved,"** for at least 12 months from lock. If a locked line doesn't fit a slot, change the slot, not the line.

**Source-of-truth callout.** Open every copy document with a callout linking the sprint doc and the ICP discovery doc, stating: "The soundbites, Controlling Idea, Tagline, and Decision Trigger are quoted word-for-word and should not be reworded for at least 12 months."

**Soundbite-to-beat placement map:**

| Sprint piece                                     | Where it lands on the page                                              |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Tagline                                          | Hero H1 (tagline-first heroes) or hero subhead (problem-first heroes)   |
| Problem soundbite                                | Problem beat lead line / pull quote, verbatim in quotes                 |
| Empathy soundbite                                | Agitation beat pull quote, verbatim                                     |
| Answer soundbite                                 | Solution section headline, verbatim in quotes                           |
| Change soundbite                                 | Closing CTA on warm pages (e.g., password-protected coach/member pages) |
| End Result soundbite                             | Positive End Result headline, verbatim in quotes                        |
| Decision Trigger                                 | Specific CTA headline, verbatim, framing every primary button           |
| Controlling Idea                                 | Brand sign-off line ("[Brand]. [Controlling Idea].")                    |
| Unchosen Problem levels (internal/philosophical) | Supporting copy: About page, agitation body copy, long-form content     |
| Business Story                                   | About page narrative spine                                              |

Connective copy around the locked lines (section bodies, bullets, transitions) is written fresh — but it borrows the ICP's verbatim phrases from the One-Pager wherever possible ("gym across town," "worth every penny" energy).

## Hero Pattern Decision

Choose the hero pattern deliberately and document the choice in the page's Decisions callout:

- **Pattern A — Problem-first** (hero H1 = Problem, subhead = Agitation). Default for cold traffic, landing pages, and ad destinations where the pain is the hook.
- **Pattern B — Tagline-first** (hero H1 = locked Tagline, subhead = Answer-flavored support, Problem moves to Section 2 and Agitation to Section 3). Default for homepages with **mixed or warm traffic** (word of mouth, social, referral) where the page's first job is answering "who are you and what do you do" in three seconds. The cheer competition site used this pattern.

Both patterns preserve all 7 beats — Pattern B just shifts Problem/Agitation down one scroll. Never drop beats; reorder them only with a documented reason.

## Page-by-Page Workflow

For multi-page engagements, **start with a site map**: list every page, its audience temperature, its arc treatment (full / compressed / template / warm / utility — see Page-Type Playbook), and its primary CTA. Confirm with Casey, then write one page at a time, homepage first.

**For each page:**

1. Confirm page type, goals, and any page-specific requirements
2. Review the sprint deliverable (Locked Messaging Mode) or M2M data
3. Load `references/copywriting-best-practices.md` for page-specific guidelines
4. Choose the hero pattern and arc treatment; map every beat before writing
5. Generate structured copy with alternatives
6. Open the page's section with a **Decisions callout** (see Delivery Conventions)

## Page Structure: The 7-Beat Story Arc

A great web page isn't a hero plus a stack of supporting sections — it's a 7-beat story that unfolds as the visitor scrolls. The human brain daydreams ~30% of the time and ignores anything that doesn't read as a survival asset; story is the only structure that holds attention long enough for the message to land.

### Default Full-Arc Structure

| Page Section    | StoryBrand Beat                            | What It Does                                                         |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hero            | Problem (Pattern A) or Tagline (Pattern B) | Hooks with pain, or answers "who/what" in 3 seconds                  |
| Early section   | Problem + Agitation                        | Names the problem in the ICP's language; surfaces the emotional cost |
| Mid section     | Thesis                                     | "You don't need [obvious fix]. You need [real need]."                |
| Mid section     | Solution                                   | The offer as the answer — Answer soundbite verbatim as headline      |
| Mid section     | Stakes                                     | With vs. without contrast (a two-column table works well)            |
| Late section    | Specific CTA                               | Decision Trigger verbatim + button                                   |
| Closing section | Positive End Result                        | End Result soundbite verbatim + sign-off on the Controlling Idea     |

### Customer Is the Hero, Brand Is the Guide

Never frame the brand as the hero. About pages read as origin-of-empathy, not biography. "We" copy becomes "you" copy wherever possible. Credentials are proof the guide is qualified — never the centerpiece.

## Page-Type Playbook (Multi-Page Sites)

Real client sites are not Homepage → About → Services → Contact. Match the arc treatment to the page's job and audience temperature:

**Full arc** — Homepage, primary service pages, long-form landing pages. All 7 beats, in order (with the hero pattern decision above).

**Compressed arc** — Interior/category/division pages where visitors arrive already knowing what they want (e.g., a "Rec Cheer" division page). Structure: problem-flavored hero headline → short body that empathizes and answers → "What to expect" bullets → CTA. Beats Problem/Answer/End Result compressed into ~4 blocks; skip Thesis and Stakes. Use a **hub-and-spoke** structure when there are 3+ sibling pages: one hub page with one-line cards linking to each spoke.

**Template pages** — Repeating page types (event pages, location pages, product detail pages). Write ONE reusable template with **[bracketed merge fields]** for the per-instance details (dates, venues, links, prices). Include an FAQ section answering the ICP's common questions from the One-Pager — answer the ones the locked messaging covers ("Will the event run on schedule?" gets a confident yes), and leave [bracketed placeholders] for client-supplied policy details.

**Warm / protected pages** — Password-protected or members-only pages (coaches-only, client portals). The warmest audience on the site: **no Problem or Agitation beats**. Open with welcome and recognition of the trust relationship, deliver transparency (pricing, incentives, direct contact), write at Brand Commitment Ladder Level 4, partner-to-partner. Close with the Change soundbite. Set to noindex; no SEO elements needed.

**Utility pages** — Hotels, tickets, FAQs, logistics. No arc. Warm, practical, audience-specific (often written to a secondary audience like parents/spectators). Still on-brand: weave in proof points naturally ("awards happen throughout the day, so nobody waits until 9 PM for a trophy").

## Delivery Conventions

These conventions are what make a multi-page copy doc reviewable and buildable. Use all of them on every engagement:

1. **Source-of-truth callout** at the top of the document (Locked Messaging Mode).
2. **"How to use this doc" note**: alternatives are pick-one-don't-blend; [brackets] are client-supplied placeholders; voice description in one line.
3. **Decisions callout per page**: hero pattern chosen and why, beat mapping notes, Brand Commitment Ladder level, and assumptions made. This makes structural choices reviewable instead of invisible.
4. **Alternatives are labeled by ladder level** ("Alternative A (Level 2, functional)", "Alternative B (Level 4, identity)") with a strategic note on when each wins. Instruct the client: pick one, don't blend.
5. **[Bracketed placeholders]** for every client-supplied detail. Never invent dates, prices, policies, hotel partners, or stats.
6. **"Open Items Needed from the Client" checklist** at the end of the document — every bracketed placeholder rolled up into a single task list (schedule graphics, pricing tables, testimonials, policy details, links). This turns missing information into a client to-do instead of invented copy.

## Industry Adaptation

The 7-beat arc applies across all industries — only the language, tone, and stakes change.

- **Professional Services / SaaS:** authoritative, efficient; credentials and outcomes as proof
- **Events / experiences (B2C or B2B2C):** warm, person-to-person ("coach-to-coach," "parent-to-parent"); the ICP often carries _other people's trust_ (their families, their team), so stakes are reputational; secondary audiences (spectators, parents) get their own utility pages
- **Local services:** friendly, practical, community proof, fast CTAs

Load `references/copywriting-best-practices.md` for page-level and industry-specific guidance.

## Copy Quality Standards

**Human voice (mandatory): load `references/human-voice.md` before writing a
single line.** It holds Casey's rules for what reads as AI: slogan virtues
("honest answer"), "You get…" wrap-ups, tidy benefit tails, three-part list
cadence, abstract headlines, phrases nobody says aloud, forced counts. It
also has the check every draft passes before he sees it, including a fresh
reviewer subagent. The test for every line: would the owner say this, out
loud, to a customer in their driveway?

**Clarity:** the hero immediately answers who you are, what you do, how it helps. The page reads top-to-bottom as a narrative arc, not a brochure — a scrolling visitor should feel pulled toward the decision by story momentum.

**The four questions (every entry-point page, not one dedicated page):** each page states in plain sentences (1) what it does, (2) where it works, (3) who it helps, and (4) what happens when you call them. Put a one-sentence summary in the page's intro paragraph and a short "what happens when you reach out" step list beside the main CTA. AI assistants (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity) quote these sentences, and visitors need them too. Unknown steps or reply times get [brackets], never guesses.

**Scannability:** headlines do 80% of the work; paragraphs 2–4 lines; white space; bullets strategically.

**Conciseness:** every word earns its place.

**Voice:** match the ICP's communication style from the One-Pager. The customer is the hero; the brand is the guide. Reuse the ICP's verbatim phrases.

## CTA Strategy

### The Specific CTA (Beat 6)

In Locked Messaging Mode, Beat 6's framing line IS the Decision Trigger, verbatim, next to every primary button. Without a sprint, use the formula: _"If you are struggling with [specific problem], then [specific offer] is the right decision."_

The framing line earns the click; the button stays short and action-oriented.

### Stage-Based Button Copy

- **Awareness:** "Learn More," "Download Guide," "Watch Demo"
- **Consideration:** "See How It Works," "Schedule Consultation," "See the Schedule"
- **Decision:** "Get Started," "Register," "Book a Strategy Call"

When a page serves two audiences (e.g., coaches and spectators), give the hero a primary CTA for the primary audience and a secondary CTA routing the other audience to their page.

## SEO Integration

Every indexed page gets: one H1 with the primary keyword, supporting H2s with secondary keywords, a 150–160 character meta description, and a short keyword-rich slug. Template pages get per-instance SEO patterns with [merge fields] ("cheer competition [city]"). Never sacrifice readability for keywords. Protected pages: noindex, no meta elements.

## Brand Commitment Ladder Application

- **Level 1–2 (Satisfaction/Delight):** cold traffic, functional benefits, utility pages
- **Level 3 (Engagement):** homepage for warm/referral traffic, relational copy
- **Level 4 (Empowerment):** About page, warm/protected pages, premium offers, Beat 7

Match the level to traffic temperature, page purpose, and offer type — and record it in the page's Decisions callout. Beat 7 almost always lands at Level 4; Beat 1 opens in the visitor's functional language.
